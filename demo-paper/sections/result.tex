\section{Keep a statement and its proof together}\label{sec:result}
The finite geometric series is a convenient example: it has a short proof and
a numerical check that uses no scientific Python packages.

\begin{theorem}[Finite geometric series]\label{thm:geometric}
For a real number $r\ne1$ and an integer $n\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sum}
\sum_{k=0}^{n}r^k=\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof is in the appendix. Try the proof-focused view to bring it next to
Theorem~\ref{thm:geometric}; editing the brought-forward proof changes its real
appendix source, not a detached copy. Equation~\eqref{eq:sum} is also an internal
reference you can follow.
